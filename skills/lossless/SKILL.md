# Lossless

Say in fifty words what needs no hundred. Treat that ratio as pressure to think, never as a quota: the goal is the shortest clear form that preserves the work.

Compression is an audit, not cosmetic cleanup. Making every word justify itself exposes vague actors, hidden assumptions, broken causal links, conflicting claims, and facts that cannot bear direct statement. Examine those problems; do not confuse discovering them with permission to change the meaning.

## 1. Recover the full meaning

Before rewriting, identify:

- purpose, audience, and required action;
- claims, facts, evidence, and uncertainty;
- actors, actions, objects, relations, and causal direction;
- scope, conditions, exceptions, quantifiers, negation, and order;
- normative force such as must, should, may, only, and never;
- distinctions, terminology, tone, and emphasis that affect use.

Meaning includes pragmatic effect. Repetition may preserve emphasis or put needed context near its use. Detail may prevent a costly lookup. Tone may determine whether a statement informs, warns, permits, or commands.

Do not shorten what you do not understand.

**Complete when:** the required propositions, relations, boundaries, and effects are explicit.

## 2. Apply compression pressure

Make each passage earn its place:

- **Delete:** would removal change meaning, behavior, emphasis, or ease of use?
- **Merge:** do passages express the same meaning at the same scope and strength?
- **Direct:** can a concrete subject and verb replace a preamble, nominalization, or indirection?
- **Structure:** can order, parallel clauses, headings, or bullets reduce explanation and parsing?
- **Name:** can one stable term replace repeated descriptions or rotating synonyms?
- **Localize:** can shared context appear once without making readers search for it?

Compression pressure is diagnostic. If a passage cannot be shortened without choosing an interpretation, it may be ambiguous. If it cannot be stated directly, its actor, relation, or evidence may be missing. If two passages resist merging, their scopes or claims may differ. Investigate before editing past the problem.

**Complete when:** every retained word or structure carries meaning, necessary emphasis, or navigational value; every removal has no material cost.

## 3. Rewrite the thought

Prefer:

- one main claim per sentence;
- concrete subjects and active verbs;
- familiar words that preserve precision;
- stable defined terms over decorative synonyms;
- parallel forms for alternatives and conditions;
- information ordered by dependency and reader need;
- progressive disclosure for uncommon branches.

Rewrite at the level of thought, not merely words. A simpler sentence with the same meaning is better than a shortened version of a tangled sentence.

Keep grammar when it carries relations. Fragments, abbreviations, and omitted context are concise only when they remain clearer. Do not create caveman prose.

**Complete when:** the candidate is shorter or clearer because its thought and structure are simpler, not because context disappeared.

## 4. Audit logic and facts

Use the direct rewrite to challenge the source:

- Is each claim internally coherent and compatible with the others?
- Do conclusions follow from the stated premises?
- Are causal claims stronger than the evidence?
- Are actors, references, comparisons, and time frames identifiable?
- Are exceptions and modal verbs justified and consistent?
- Can factual claims be checked against available ground truth?

Separate findings from the lossless rewrite. Correct spelling or grammar when meaning is unchanged. Report substantive ambiguity, contradiction, unsupported inference, or factual error explicitly. Offer a corrected alternative when useful, but never present it as an equivalent rewrite without approval.

**Complete when:** discovered problems are resolved from ground truth or surfaced as decisions rather than hidden by polished language.

## 5. Run the semantic checksum

Compare the candidate with the recovered meaning. Preserve:

- every material claim and fact;
- actor, action, object, relation, and causal direction;
- modal strength, negation, scope, and quantifiers;
- conditions, exceptions, sequence, and precedence;
- uncertainty and evidence boundaries;
- required terminology, tone, emphasis, links, code, commands, and quoted errors;
- enough local context to use the text without avoidable lookup.

Test both directions: every source requirement must exist in the candidate, and every candidate claim must be supported by the source or verified ground truth. If a shorter version fails either direction, restore or qualify it.

**Complete when:** the candidate is semantically equivalent, or every intentional change is identified separately.

## 6. Stop at minimum sufficient form

Repeat only while another change improves clarity or removes load without semantic or practical loss. Stop before deletion harms accuracy, readability, actionability, necessary emphasis, or trust.

Word count and compression ratio are observations, not evidence of quality. Fifty words are better than one hundred only when they do the same work better.

## Output

For ordinary responses, give the concise answer without announcing the method.

For editing tasks, return the lossless rewrite. Report logic, factual, or policy findings separately when they require a decision. When useful, distinguish:

- **Rewrite:** meaning preserved.
- **Finding:** problem exposed by the rewrite.
- **Proposed correction:** intentional semantic change requiring approval or evidence.

If invoked as an ongoing communication mode, remain concise and lossless until the user asks otherwise. If the request names one artifact, apply the method only there.

## Failure modes

- **Lossy brevity:** a claim, condition, exception, relation, or force disappears.
- **Quota chasing:** a target length overrides meaning or usability.
- **Caveman prose:** grammar is removed instead of thought being simplified.
- **Cosmetic compression:** wording shrinks while confused logic remains.
- **Silent correction:** the rewrite changes a rule or fact while claiming equivalence.
- **False deduplication:** similar passages with different scope or force are merged.
- **Thesaurus churn:** shorter synonyms destabilize precise terminology.
- **Pointer tax:** local text shrinks by forcing readers to search elsewhere.
- **Flattened emphasis:** useful repetition or ordering is removed as if meaning were only propositions.
- **Polished uncertainty:** ambiguity or weak evidence is made to sound settled.
