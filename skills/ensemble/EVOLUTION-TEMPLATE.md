# Evolution readings

An evolution reading explains how evidence changed a design or working method so later readers can learn and challenge it. It is distinct from a current checkpoint, a chronological activity list, and a catalog of reusable lessons.

With managed plugin support, supply the fields below through the reflection tools. The plugin assigns entry identities and ordering, checks references, manages review/publication, and renders ordinary Markdown outside the workspace. The model does not create directories, edit generated documents, allocate numbers, or run Git. A skill-only workflow can use the same questions in its isolated records repository.

## Questions for one proposed entry

- **Starting problem:** Which concrete uncertainty, mistaken assumption, or incomplete result mattered?
- **Action and evidence:** What was actually inspected, attempted, compared, or verified? Link the observed artifact and revision. Distinguish user reports, source reading, executed checks, and untested proposals.
- **Change in judgment:** What changed, what was rejected or retained, and why did that evidence justify it?
- **Lesson and limits:** What should a later worker do differently, under which conditions? What counterexample or missing evidence limits this advice?
- **Next test:** What new observation could support or overturn the lesson? Do not count an acknowledged instruction as demonstrated improvement.

Use a title naming the learning point, not a tool call. One meaningful entry may cover parallel work. Routine status updates and edits with no decision-changing result need no story.

## Ordering, review, and corrections

Published entries use sequence numbers without dates, clock times, or work durations as narrative metadata. Preserve measurements or validity conditions when they are substantive evidence, clearly distinguished from a work timeline. Git and native execution records retain their normal operational metadata; do not copy them into narrative headings or invent a precise chronology. On initial backfill, say which discussions and artifacts were available and that the sequence is a reconstruction, not a precise edit timeline.

A non-author checks important causal claims against the cited material before publication; the Lead owns the final editorial decision. An unchecked draft remains a draft, and a lesson without transfer evidence remains a hypothesis. A review may reject an overgeneralization even if every link exists.

Preserve failed approaches. Correct a published entry with an explicit amendment and reason, maintaining its stable identity and prior revision. Do not rewrite the past to suggest the right answer was known from the start; do not fabricate unavailable conversations, motivations, runs, or results.

## Divide by subject when it improves reading

Keep a short overview of major turns and cross-domain consequences. Place deeper sequences under stable subject collections when readers need different expertise or the text no longer supports focused reading. Give a shared turning point one primary home; other domains reference its identity and explain their own consequence instead of copying its story. Moving or splitting a collection preserves entry links and ordering; folder names alone do not define domain ownership.

A reusable lesson is a separately assessed takeaway with a trigger, action, evidence, counterexamples, applicability, and invalidation conditions. Retrieve only lessons relevant to the next task; an archive full of prose is not automatically a useful memory system.
